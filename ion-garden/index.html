 THE GARDENER’S POCKET GUIDE

A little help.
A lot of protection.

⌂

One shared memory

The three slimes in a habitat represent three ion qubits. Together they hold one encoded memory, not three independent copies.

✦

Helpers find clues

Two helper slimes check whether pairs agree, without reading the memory. Their clue is called a syndrome. A decoder chooses the correction automatically.

❋

Make room for care

Each lantern serves one habitat at a time. Waiting lets errors accumulate. Dew coolers improve the reliability of operations nearby.

✿

Grow at your pace

Plant equipment, accept contracts, and spend earned seeds. Balanced checks are a good start. Frequent checks may cause queues; gentle checks leave longer gaps.

Slimes, gardens, and seeds are fiction. Traps hold real ions; lasers control their qubit states. This game models repeated correction of bit flips with a three-qubit repetition code. It does not protect against phase errors or demonstrate full fault tolerance. Errors are hidden until delivery; a quiet syndrome does not prove a memory is intact.
