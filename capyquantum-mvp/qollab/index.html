Encoding is not copying.

We map α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ into α|000⟩ + β|111⟩. The information lives in a joint state, not three independent copies.

A clue, not a confession.

Two ancillas record whether pairs disagree. For these selected Pauli errors, the syndrome identifies a possible bit-flip location without revealing α or β.

Protection has a boundary.

This code corrects one X error. It does not protect arbitrary phase errors or multiple bit flips. All encoding and recovery gates here are ideal.

FIELD NOTES

Your five-minute field guide.

A logical qubit is the information you want to protect. Three data qubits hold its encoded state. Two extra ancillas carry error clues.

X swaps |0⟩ and |1⟩. Z changes the relative sign of the |1⟩ amplitude. Y combines both effects, up to a global phase.

Fidelity measures how closely the recovered quantum state matches the input: 100% means the same state, including phase. It is a simulator diagnostic, not a reading you can obtain from one unknown physical qubit.

Z-basis measurement asks about |0⟩ / |1⟩. X-basis measurement asks about |+⟩ / |−⟩. Both views help expose a hidden phase change.

This lab uses coherent recovery: the ancillas control correction gates, then the logical state is decoded into q0. Measuring the syndrome can be deferred until the end. We do not model realistic hardware noise or claim fault tolerance.

The browser provides ideal statevector diagnostics. The Qollab button submits one X-basis circuit to your selected free simulator; sampled counts are separate from ideal fidelity. Rerun the project to submit another job.
